Method

Two-stage model. A Random Forest classifier (best of four by F1) estimates the probability of a wet day (≥ 1 mm, WMO threshold). For wet days, an Extra Trees regressor trained on log-transformed rainfall predicts intensity, calibrated by empirical quantile mapping. Features: temperature, relative humidity, surface pressure, specific humidity, wind speed and direction, moisture flux and thermal instability.

Live inputs. Daily means from Open-Meteo; specific humidity derived from dew point and pressure; 10 m wind scaled to 2 m (×0.72).

Scenarios. SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5 projections come from the study's CMIP6-ML pipeline (regional warming rates with Clausius–Clapeyron moisture scaling).

Limitations

• Occurrence is predicted well (ROC-AUC ≈ 0.94), but daily rainfall amount has little skill from these predictors alone (R² ≈ 0.01); treat intensity as a typical value for a wet day, not a precise forecast.
• Tree models cannot extrapolate beyond the conditions seen in training, so predicted intensities are compressed and extreme events are under-represented.
• Projected anomalies are relative to the observed baseline and include model bias; the change from near-term to late-century (shown under Projections) isolates the scenario-driven signal.
• Live inputs come from a different source than the training data. This site provides statistical estimates, not official forecasts.
